# Humiliation

## 1. Section overview
This category concerns Families, schools, workplaces or online communities.

## 2. What this section covers
The section explains recognition, common patterns, immediate safety, support and reporting choices, prevention and recovery.

## 3. Who it applies to
- A survivor, friend, colleague, educator or support worker.
- It also applies to friends, families, neighbors, teachers, employers, clinicians and safeguarding or advocacy professionals.

## 4. Severity and urgency
- Repeated humiliation can cause severe psychological harm even without physical injury.
- **Low, medium or high risk cannot be determined only by the absence of visible injury.** Repeated threats, confinement, coercion, isolation or loss of essential support may be urgent.
- Use immediate emergency services for active danger, serious injury, a child or adult unable to leave, or a credible threat.

## 5. Core safeguards
- Prioritize immediate safety and emergency help when harm is active or likely to become life-threatening.
- Use clear boundaries, informed consent, private communication options and an independent advocate.
- Preserve records securely; do not share a survivor's private account or contact details without permission.
- Arrange appropriate safeguarding, healthcare, legal or financial support and check in after reporting.
- For institutional or repeated harm, use independent oversight and protect complainants from retaliation.

## 6. Key takeaways
- Abuse is behavior, not a survivor's fault.
- Listen without blame, protect privacy and offer choices.
- Patterns, fear and loss of autonomy are important even when evidence is incomplete.
- Safety planning should be personalized because every relationship, location and legal system differs.

## 7. Warning signs
- Sudden fear, withdrawal, secrecy, restricted movement or communication, unexplained injuries or needs, threats, or a rapid loss of money or belongings.
- Dependence, unusual control, inconsistent explanations or an inability to speak freely may indicate concern.
- A person's response or delayed disclosure can reflect fear and should not be treated as proof that the report is false.

## 8. Immediate response
- Move to a safe place when possible and contact trained local support.
- Ask what the person wants and what they need for immediate safety.
- Record factual observations and preserve original records only through safe and lawful means.
- Do not confront a suspected abuser, remove a vulnerable person, or conduct an unofficial investigation.

## 9. Helplines and NGOs
- **India - emergency:** call **112** for police, fire or ambulance. For women's safety support, use **181**; verify the locally available service.
- **India - child protection:** call **1098** (Childline) or use local child-safeguarding procedures. Emergency danger is reported through **112**.
- **India - older adults:** seek local police, social-welfare or emergency services. Helpage India may help at **1800-180-1253**; confirm availability before reliance.
- **International:** contact local emergency services. RAINN (US/Canada resources: https://rainn.org; US hotline 800-656-4673), the UN trafficking information portal (https://www.unodc.org), or Child Helpline International (https://childhelplineinternational.org) can help locate support.
- **NGOs:** UNICEF (https://www.unicef.org), UN Women (https://www.unwomen.org), Tulir (https://www.tulir.org), CRY (https://cry.org), Human Rights Watch (https://www.hrw.org), and The Hotline (https://www.thehotline.org) provide safeguarding, rights or referral resources.

## 10. Reporting and professional support
- Options include police or emergency services, healthcare, safeguarding teams, advocates, community organizations, legal aid and jurisdiction-specific adult or child protection agencies.
- Before disclosure, ask a professional which confidentiality limits or mandatory-reporting duties apply.
- Reporting choices should preserve the person's safety and, when lawful, their voice and autonomy.

## 11. Prevention
- Build consent, privacy, financial independence, accessible complaint routes and regular safeguarding reviews into homes, schools and organizations.
- Train staff and families to recognize warning signs and prevent retaliation.
- Require independent oversight where a person has limited mobility, capacity or access to support.
